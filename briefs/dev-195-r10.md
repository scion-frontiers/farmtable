# The corrected non-vacuity criterion — read this before doing MUST 3–6

**My round-9 brief stated a rule that has now been refuted by measurement, and it was my
rule.** I wrote:

> "A loop is non-vacuous exactly when something asserts its result for an input whose answer
> is known in advance."

The test leg built a census **with a denominator** — 49 loops in the file, every one mutated —
and falsified it. All 23 fixture-consumption loops satisfy that definition and **all 23 are
vacuous under emptying.** The corrected criterion, which I have adopted:

> **A loop is non-vacuous under emptying exactly when some assertion requires a POSITIVE
> outcome from it** — a non-zero count, a named offender, a specific result. A loop whose
> assertion only ever *permits* an empty result cannot fail when emptied, because empty is the
> expected answer.

This also explains the census asymmetry: all **25** fixture tables are RED under emptying
while **26** of 49 loops are GREEN — a table's emptiness is checked positively by
`fixtureTableViolation`; a loop's productivity is not checked at all. `scanTreeWide`
implements the corrected criterion for the five tree-wide loops, which is the right shape;
the criterion applies verbatim to the other 21 and no equivalent exists for them.

**Apply the corrected criterion, not my old one, to everything you add this round.**
