## B-1 retracted — and how the error was made

The eng-manager challenged B-1 and pre-registered a hypothesis: that my M1 run used a ten-test
`-run` filter which **excluded `TestPassthroughReadDropsUnsafeRemoteURL`**. It did. The filter was
`^(...)$`-anchored over ten names; that test could not have matched.

Re-applying M1 and running that one test: **RED on all six hostile-scheme subtests.** The `labels`
carrier **is** pinned. Five candidate explanations for the original green were eliminated by
measurement — mutant-didn't-take, test cache, third carrier, assertion-never-ran, and wrong-tree
(path + SHA + `git diff` printed in the same command as the run).

Two things I got wrong, not one:

1. **The wide claim.** My narrow finding — no test *names* `issueBuildRemoteData` or `issueLabels` —
   was true. I restated it as "nothing goes red if the carrier changes" **without re-measuring at the
   boundary**, and the disconfirming fact was sitting in my own "what I did not check" list.
2. **The direction of alarm.** Under M1 the sanitizer strips `remote_url` and `html_url`
   end-to-end — 7 fields ship, neither of them a URL. M1 is **not** a live XSS. I overstated the
   danger as well as the gap.

**Method lesson, and it is the same lapse twice.** Earlier in this round I ran a guessed `-run`
filter that returned "no tests to run" and I correctly refused to read that as "no test catches it."
An hour later I made exactly that inference from a `-run` filter that was too narrow. Recognising
the failure mode in the small case did not stop me repeating it in the large one. **A green under a
name-filtered run is evidence about the filter until the filter is shown to contain the relevant
test.**

Two of three legs agreed with me, and both had reached the conclusion by the same method — a name
search in test files. **Agreement between two legs sharing one method is one piece of evidence, not
two.** The leg that tested the boundary beat both.

## A hazard this round surfaced that our mutation rules do not cover

`buildRemoteData` in `github.go` writes `rd["labels"]` **guarded by `if len(labelNames) > 0`**.
Against a zero-label fixture, deleting that line removes code that never executed and reports green.
**A mutant that deletes dead code is indistinguishable from a surviving mutant in the exit code.**

Proposed rule: *a mutant must be shown to be live before its survival is reported* — via a printed
side effect, a coverage line, or a positive control. I audited my own eight mutants against it and
found **0 no-ops**, but only after measuring the one genuinely at risk (M2's `log.Printf`, which
fires only on a non-nil `structpb` error). It executes once at baseline, driven by
`TestMapStringStringStaysUnrepresentable_GuardsO1`. **B-2 stands on measured ground.**

By that same rule, `sub_issues` is **UNMEASURED, not cleared**: the fixture sets `subIssues.nodes: []`,
so that branch never runs.
