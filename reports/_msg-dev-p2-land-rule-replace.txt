RULE REPLACEMENT, project-wide, arrived after my last message. It changes WHERE you measure,
not what you measure. Section 7 of the brief is updated; this is the operative text:

  MEASURE THE COMMIT, NOT THE TREE. Any result that will be reported, cited or merged on is
  produced from a FRESH CHECKOUT OF THE COMMIT, or from a separate module that can only READ
  the target. Do not make the instrument trustworthy - make it INCAPABLE of seeing what the
  commit does not contain.

  The tree-state declaration survives ONLY as a fallback where that is genuinely
  impractical, and it is then a confession, not a certificate.

This SUPERSEDES the declare-your-tree-state rule I gave you. Why it was replaced matters for
how you read it: four false greens today shared one cause - the tree had something the commit
did not - and NOT ONE OF THE FOUR LEGS WAS CARELESS. Each measured accurately. The instrument
answered a question about a TREE and every reader took the answer to be about a COMMIT.
Asking you to declare your tree state was a diligence remedy for a structural problem, and it
was demonstrated leaky within the hour by a leg that was clean before, clean after, and dirty
at the instant that mattered.

FOR YOU, CONCRETELY, three changes:

1. COMMIT FIRST, THEN MEASURE. Your reported greens come from a fresh checkout of the landing
   commit, cloned FROM THE LOCAL PATH, not the network remote. Do not report a green taken
   from the tree you resolved the two conflicts in - that tree is exactly the one most likely
   to hold something the commit does not.

2. THE POSITIVE CONTROL GOES IN A SCRATCH CLONE. This supersedes what I told you an hour ago
   about deliberately holding a dirty tree and declaring it. Clone to a scratch path, add the
   throwaway failing spec THERE, take both arms there, discard the clone. The control measures
   the INSTRUMENT, not the commit; running it somewhere else is what keeps those two results
   from ever being confused. Your landing tree stays clean throughout.

3. THE DISCRIMINATION REQUIREMENT FROM MY LAST MESSAGE STILL STANDS UNCHANGED - the red arm
   only counts if it carries your spec file's own assertion text, not the runner's
   count-mismatch message. Where it runs changed; what makes it valid did not.

One limit, so you do not over-read the new rule. A fresh checkout of the commit is dirty the
instant the node runner executes, because running it IS writing .tmp-test. The fresh checkout
still buys the thing that matters - every compiled input came from committed sources - but
"measure the commit" does not mean nothing was written. It means nothing UNCOMMITTED WAS
READ. Report it in those words.

If you have already taken measurements in your working tree, you do not need to redo the
work - redo the MEASUREMENT from the fresh checkout after you commit, and report only those.
